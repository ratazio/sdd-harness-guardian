Why this correction matters

A stakeholder page is an authority surface. If it says “candidate” while canonical state says rendered, a reader cannot know which record governs. The required outcome is a promotion that proves its actual lifecycle in both source bindings and visible language, while leaving domain meaning to humans. ## Objective and outcome

In scope

Closed lifecycle markers; synchronized phase and state digest; recoverable promotion; byte/identity checks; and separate pre- versus post-render review.

Deliberately out

**Non-goals:** changing domain semantics, replacing SPEC 021’s human hook, or letting a deterministic pass authorize a baseline.

Lifecycle architecture: a narrow, auditable rewrite

The renderer is not a content editor. It can derive only declared lifecycle values from staged rendered state, including declared repeatable authority projections. All other authored bytes, including decision prose and ordinary source provenance, remain outside its rewrite surface.

1. Composition review

A reviewed composition declares the closed lifecycle-marker set and binds material sections to current source bytes.

2. Stage

Create in-memory rendered state and derive the allowlisted marker values only.

3. Validate

Reject unknown, duplicate, misplaced or non-lifecycle declarations before exposure.

4. Commit pair

Use temporary files, journal and backups; rename state and target in a recoverable order.

5. Human review

Review the exact rendered page after promotion; do not inherit composition-review authority.

Interrupted-pair decision model

The unit of recovery is one pair: the rendered HTML and run-state.yaml. Before promotion, the prior pair remains the only visible pair. The journal records the intended digests for both new members, the prior pair’s digests or absence, and the two common-nonce temporary names.

What recovery finds Required decision What may be exposed
Prior HTML and state; no journal. No promotion is pending. The prior complete pair.
Journal present, with complete temps and intent digests; backups or renames may be partial. Do not infer success from rename order. Compare both destinations with the intended digests. Nothing while the journal remains.
Both destinations exactly match the new HTML and state digests. Complete recovery by removing journal, backups and temps. The new pair, only after cleanup.
Either destination does not match the intended digest. Restore each prior member from backup, or remove it if it did not previously exist. Nothing until the prior pair is intact.
A required backup is absent and its destination does not match the recorded prior digest; or journal schema, names, nonce or metadata are unsafe. Refuse automatic recovery and preserve suspicious artifacts for manual intervention. Nothing.

Promotion writes temporary files, makes the journal durable, backs up the old HTML and state, installs the new state first and the new HTML second, then cleans up. A future promotion of a reviewed exact candidate atomically makes brief_phase: rendered and current_phase: rendered_decision_review_pending. Recovery runs before any later promotion. Therefore the sole exposure point for a new brief is a complete digest-validated pair with no pending journal.

Impact, data boundary and risk controls

The affected system is the Guardian rendering boundary—renderer, state, templates and validation—not a consumer implementation. The material data boundary is canonical lifecycle state and its digest; arbitrary domain prose is explicitly not transformable.

Promotion renderer

| Promotion renderer stages rendered state and allowlisted marker values through journal, backup and recovery before target write.

Provenance

Each material source block identifies its source, section, coverage disposition, whole-file digest, visible fragment and fragment digest. The renderer may refresh only the declared final-state provenance block.

Human Visibility

A distinct rendered-decision review owns the page’s adequacy after promotion. Deterministic checks prove bindings; they do not score quality or materiality.

Primary failure signal. | IR-022-01 is a rendered HTML/current-state mismatch or surviving journal. The owner’s control is temporary files, journal/backup recovery and regression coverage; an interrupted promotion must repair or safely refuse before a page is exposed.

Decision-prose boundary

| IR-022-02 |: signal is domain text changing without source provenance or an unknown/duplicate marker. The control is a closed lifecycle-marker schema and reviewer; V-022-01/04 validates that the transformer cannot edit arbitrary decision prose.

Review-authority boundary

| IR-022-03 |: signal is missing or distorted rendered-review metadata. A distinct reviewer/record, owned by the delivery lead, prevents pre-render approval from being mistaken for post-render authority; V-022-05 is the validation.

Validation and honest oracle boundary

The proof plan maps acceptance criteria to deterministic lifecycle checks and, separately, to people who judge the rendered decision page. The machine oracle binds bytes, identity and lifecycle; it cannot decide whether prose is sufficient or material.

V-022-03 / 04

Inject faults at every transaction boundary, recover or refuse safely, and prove the implementation contains no domain taxonomy, prose score or semantic approval.

V-022-05a / 05b

SPEC 021 requires a fresh, bound composition review and then five distinct post-render lenses, all bound to exact digests; any material revise blocks gates.

Required regressions: renderer, source-render isolation, Human Visibility, decision-quality and full bundle checks. Structural PASS is necessary evidence, not stakeholder approval.

Recovery operation. **Rollback/recovery:** a commit boundary leaves journal and backup sufficient to restore the old pair or finish only a digest-validated new pair. Historical artifacts require explicit refresh; they are never silently overwritten.
